Make every word count: styling and correcting your captions

Fix transcript mistakes, choose a readable style and use word-by-word highlighting with care.

Snipo Clips guides · Updated 10 October 2026
IN THIS GUIDECorrect the words before stylingChoose a style that serves the footageApply and review the rendered result

Correct the words before styling

Captions are part of your message. Open a generated folder, select Edit captions and read the transcript while watching the clip. Pay extra attention to names, numbers, technical terms and words spoken over noise. Correct the wording in the text editor before choosing the final visual style.

Text correction edits the existing transcript; it is not a translation tool. The caption editor needs the clip’s editable master and word timings. Some older clips may not have those assets. If the editor reports that the transcript is unavailable, regenerate from your source instead of expecting styling alone to recreate the missing text.

Choose a style that serves the footage

Start with a preset, then adjust color, size and position. Readability matters more than the number of effects. Use contrast against the actual video, leave room around the speaker’s face and check where a platform’s controls might overlap your caption. Preview the result before applying it.

Karaoke captions highlight the active word using its timing. This can help viewers follow a fast explanation, but a calmer caption style may suit reflective moments. Pause and inspect a few frames to check timing and legibility. When you correct the transcript, review the timing again, particularly if the number of words changes.

Apply and review the rendered result

A preview helps you compare your choices. Apply the edit to render the updated clip, then play the finished result and check that your wording, colors and position survived the export. Existing videos need to be rendered again before a new caption style becomes part of their saved output.

Download your preferred version before its retention period ends. If you continue in the Video Studio, review the Studio’s own caption settings as well; the final exported video is the source of truth for what a viewer will see.
